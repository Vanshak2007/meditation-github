THE BIOLOGY OF CALM

What happens inside your body?

Stress is not just a thought. It can involve your brain, nervous system, breathing, heart rate and muscles. Understanding these connections can help you choose useful coping strategies.

⚡

Fight-or-flight response

The sympathetic nervous system helps prepare your body for action. During stress, heart rate and breathing may increase, and muscles may tense.

SYMPATHETIC SYSTEM
🌿

Rest-and-digest

The parasympathetic nervous system supports functions such as digestion and recovery. Comfortable, unhurried breathing may help support relaxation.

PARASYMPATHETIC SYSTEM
🧪

Stress hormones

The body's stress response involves hormones such as adrenaline and cortisol. Relaxation practices may help manage stress, but they do not instantly switch off every stress response.

HORMONAL RESPONSE
🫁

Breathing and the body

Slow, comfortable breathing may support a relaxation response. Keep your breath natural and stop the exercise if you feel dizzy or uncomfortable.

BREATH AWARENESS
🎯

Attention and awareness

Mindfulness involves noticing thoughts, feelings and sensations, then gently bringing attention back to the present without harsh self-judgment.

MINDFUL ATTENTION
🌙

Sleep and recovery

A regular wind-down routine, relaxing activities and a consistent sleep schedule can support healthy rest and everyday functioning.

REST AND RECOVERY
Explore the science behind stress and relaxation

The autonomic nervous system regulates many automatic body functions. The sympathetic and parasympathetic branches have different but interacting roles. Relaxation techniques can support wellbeing, although the effects differ between people. Meditation should complement, not replace, appropriate healthcare.

YOUR BRAIN CAN ADAPT

Welcome to the neuroplasticity lab

Learning is not limited to classrooms. Repeated experiences and practice help the brain adapt throughout life.

🧠
Practice builds skills
Learning takes time

Train a habit, not perfection.

Neuroplasticity is the nervous system's ability to change its functioning and connections in response to experience, learning and practice. Meditation is one possible way to practise attention and awareness.

  • ✓ Attention practice: notice when your mind wanders and gently return to your chosen focus.
  • ✓ Emotional awareness: identify what you are feeling before deciding how to respond.
  • ✓ Skill development: practise a hobby regularly and learn from small mistakes.
  • ✓ Consistency over intensity: begin with a manageable routine and adjust it to suit your needs.

Important: Neuroplasticity does not mean that meditation automatically rewires the brain or eliminates anxiety. Brain research is ongoing, and results vary.

YOUR EVERYDAY TOOLKIT

Small actions for difficult moments

Explore practical techniques and choose what feels comfortable. You do not need to do everything at once.

🖐️ The 5–4–3–2–1 grounding exercise

Gently bring attention to your surroundings:

  1. Notice five things you can see.
  2. Notice four things you can feel.
  3. Notice three things you can hear.
  4. Notice two things you can smell, if available.
  5. Notice one thing you can taste, if available.

Adapt or skip any step that feels uncomfortable.

📝 The thought-unloading page

Try this simple journaling structure:

  1. What is taking up space in my mind?
  2. What part of this can I influence?
  3. What is one small action I can take?
  4. What can wait until another day?
💪 Progressive muscle relaxation

In a comfortable position, gently tense a muscle group for a few seconds and release it. Move through your hands, shoulders and legs if that feels suitable.

Avoid tensing painful or injured areas. You can simply notice and release tension instead.

📵 A digital sunset

Try a gentle evening reset:

  1. Silence nonessential notifications.
  2. Put your phone away for a short period.
  3. Choose a book, relaxing music or a hobby.
  4. Keep a regular bedtime where possible.
🌱 Build a tiny habit

Connect a new habit to an existing routine. For example, take three comfortable mindful breaths after brushing your teeth or write one journal sentence after dinner.

Missing a day is normal. Return when you can rather than aiming for perfection.

🤝 Reach out instead of isolating

Send a message to someone you trust, take a walk with a friend or join a group around a hobby you enjoy.

If anxiety or stress keeps affecting everyday life, a qualified mental health professional can help you explore additional support.
